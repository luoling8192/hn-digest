# Channel feedback and private reading

## Scope

Channel likes and saves are open to every reader. Each article has `♡ 喜欢 N` and `☆ 收藏 N` callback buttons; clicking again cancels that reader's state. Counts show accounts currently liking or saving that article, not click totals. The shared keyboard displays aggregate counts; a private callback notification confirms the acting reader's state. Individual saved lists remain private. The title links to the original article; the separate original-URL line is omitted from the channel message. Private `/start`, `/help`, `/recommend`, `/saved`, `推荐`, `再来`, `给我推荐`, `我的收藏`, and `收藏夹` are public. Public readers can select articles, save, request more or fewer similar articles, clear selection, undo, open their saved list, and paginate or remove saved articles. Callbacks require the reader's own private chat, owned batch, and exact batch message. Public recommendation and saved-list controls contain no article-chat or natural-language preference controls.

Public recommendations rank articles already stored in the reading library and reuse published channel metadata. They make no model requests, source fetches, or automatic deep-page jobs. Titles link to an existing Chinese page when available, otherwise to the original article. Personal likes, saves, fewer-like-this feedback, and exposure history affect ranking. Article questions, deep reading, and natural-language preference changes still require `TELEGRAM_OWNER_ID` or an explicit owner grant. Only the configured owner can see operations data and manage AI access. Group messages, forwarded buttons in other chats, and another user's batch callbacks cannot access profiles. Article sources and generated pages are shared; reader feedback and conversation history remain personal. Public recommendation, likes, and saves never grant AI or administrative access.

Open `/status` → “AI 功能授权” → “添加 AI 用户 UID”, then send the user's numeric Telegram UID. The owner can also use `/users`, `/allow UID`, and `/deny UID`. Disabling retains the profile and bookmarks and blocks subsequent AI requests and their responses; public recommendations, likes, and saved-list access remain available. The owner cannot be disabled through this list. Grants persist in SQLite across deployments and do not grant management rights. Users must initiate private chat themselves; `/id` reveals their own UID without granting AI access. An in-flight model request may finish after access is disabled, but its response will not be delivered. AI-enabled users share the configured OpenRouter account budget; there is no per-user AI billing or AI daily budget in this release.

The reader gets up to five articles per message. Inline number buttons select articles; selection does not alter preference weights. Save, more-like-this, fewer-like-this, cancel, and undo edit the existing message. Saved articles paginate in place. New recommendations append a new batch. Replying to a previous batch uses that batch's numbering. Unresolvable replies ask for the target batch instead of using the latest one.

Public commands: `/start`, `/recommend`, `/saved`, `/help`, and their documented Chinese equivalents. `/start saved` opens the saved list directly. AI-enabled readers can additionally use `/preferences`, natural-language preference changes, and article questions grounded in stored source text. Their recommended articles are queued for full Telegraph summaries with separate, cited HN discussion sections (two concurrent jobs, at most ten queued). The list appears immediately; links are updated in place as summaries become ready. “聊聊这篇” reuses or creates that cached page on demand. Public readers do not start either generation path, and neither path publishes to the channel.

## Shared feedback counts

Counts come directly from persisted reader profiles, including historical feedback, with each account contributing at most one like and one save per article. Channel toggles, private feedback, removal, negative opinions, and revision-aware undo update the same state. Profile writes atomically mark a fixed per-article synchronization record pending. Acknowledgements preserve its monotonically increasing version and cannot clear changes made during an outstanding Telegram request. No schema migration or accumulated click counter is needed.

A background task refreshes only the canonical published channel message and explicitly bound dev previews, using `editMessageReplyMarkup`. It acknowledges clicks before count edits and leaves normal polling responsive. Body and keyboard edits share a per-message queue, and body edits read current counts when they execute. Initial rollout queues existing published messages once per configured channel. New publications render current counts immediately. Interactive changes and newly bound previews take priority over historical backfill, so new clicks do not wait behind old messages. Each synchronization pass processes at most 20 pending versions; subsequent polling continues the backlog.

One target failing does not block another target or article. Failed articles remain pending, wait at least 30 seconds before another attempt in that process, and retry on later polling; pending state survives restart. Counts can therefore lag while Telegram is unavailable. Shutdown aborts background edits without clearing pending state and drains publishing before closing either SQLite store. No reader identity or saved-list content is included in the shared keyboard.

## Public limits

Public readers can receive one recommendation batch every 30 seconds and at most 20 batches per UTC+8 calendar day. A delivered, nonempty batch counts toward that quota; empty results and failed delivery do not. Saved-list reads, selection, feedback, and undo do not consume recommendation-batch quota, but still pass through action limits.

Public actions use persistent token buckets: each reader refills at 30 actions per minute with a burst capacity of 10; all public readers share a bucket refilling at 300 actions per minute with a burst capacity of 10. These are refill rates rather than strict rolling-window counts. Rejected actions do not change feedback, consume a batch, or enter model work. Rejection text is limited to once per reader every 30 seconds; callbacks are still acknowledged when a repeated notice is suppressed. Limits and daily counters survive restart. The owner and AI-enabled readers retain their existing authorized path and are not subject to these public-reader limits. This is request admission control, not a Telegram outbound queue or an AI spending cap.

## Account and storage

`/data/reading.sqlite` is separate from the existing delivery database. It stores per-user profiles, stable recommendation/saved batches, bookmark/opinion state, source text, article metadata, channel callback receipts, optional dev-message bindings, and the Telegram polling offset. Both files use the existing `/data` volume and WAL. No existing publication table or schema version is changed. A channel click commits its user state, source metadata, and callback receipt in one transaction; replaying that same callback does not toggle again, including after restart. Distinct clicks toggle state independently. Newer feedback revisions survive an in-flight model operation saving an older profile snapshot.

Feedback is a state per reader/article, not an accumulated click counter. Bookmark weight is +1, explicit opinion is +3 or -3, distributed over the article's topics. Explicit interests are stronger. Undo checks a per-article revision so it does not overwrite a newer action. A rolling 2,000-article exposure history prevents immediate repeats. The ranking combines topic affinity, modest log-scaled HN score, and topic diversity. It excludes disliked, saved, already shown, and title-only articles. Source years and raw HN scores remain visible; scores are not presented as year-normalized popularity.

## Historical library

1. Index up to 100 high-score stories from each of the preceding 60 monthly windows using the public HN Algolia API, requiring at least 150 points. This aims for approximately 6,000 candidates, but records without external article links are excluded, so the actual count is lower.
2. Prioritize architecture, startup and engineering-experience candidates by title keywords and the least-covered topic. Retrieve at most three concurrently. Failed/challenge sources are retained as failed candidates and never promoted based on comments or title alone.
3. Ground a Chinese introduction and reusable topics in retrieved source text, with an explicit readability check. Retain the extracted source, length, reading estimate, and truncation marker. A successful extraction does not guarantee every element of the original website was captured; the prompt sees at most 14,000 source characters.
4. Prepare `READING_ARCHIVE_TARGET` successful historical articles initially (default 300). As articles are shown, replenish toward a cap of 500. Resume automatically after restart using persisted index and candidate status. Transient failed candidates are eligible after seven days.

The archive worker runs within the persistent Railway service. It is independent of private-chat polling and channel publishing. Model requests use the existing OpenRouter key and its configured account budget. Completing the initial target is not proof of recommendation quality. No large backfill completion is claimed until `/admin/reading` reports actual counts.

Deep summaries retain the publisher's evidence validation. Authorized deep-page generation allows one corrective model call when citation or discussion-evidence validation fails; a second invalid result remains rejected. Failed pages retain the original article link. Restart resumes unfinished deep pages for AI-enabled readers' latest recommendation batches without sending another list. Public batches are not automatically prepared after restart.

## Operation and verification

Send `/status`, `运行状态`, or `状态面板` in the owner's private chat to open the operations panel. It shows automatic publishing mode, the active publication stage and story ID, last completed cycle, publication and unresolved-delivery counts, historical indexing progress (out of 60 months), parsed articles versus the current target, readable/deep-page counts, and recent publication timestamps. Times are shown in UTC+8. The panel does not invoke the model or start work.

“刷新状态” edits the same message with a fresh snapshot. “查看失败” shows up to five current channel failure records, their error codes, attempt counts and earliest retry times; archive failures are counted separately in the overview. These are current records rather than lifetime statistics. An elapsed retry delay does not guarantee publication: unpublished stories must still appear in the HN top 30. `sending` and `uncertain` records are shown as deliveries requiring inspection, not safe-to-retry work. Only the configured owner in their own private chat can read either view.

The panel is an on-demand snapshot, not an independent uptime monitor: if the service is down it cannot respond, and Telegram polling may wait for a preceding reading operation to finish. The owner's `/help` lists status and access commands.

Omit `TELEGRAM_OWNER_ID` to disable polling, channel feedback buttons, private chat, and the archive worker. With an owner configured, public feedback is enabled for every reader independently of the AI grants. No webhook may be registered for the token when long polling is used. Run one service replica with zero deployment overlap. Claimed work is drained before the database is closed.

For dev acceptance with the same bot, optionally configure a separate `TELEGRAM_PREVIEW_CHAT_ID`. Publish or edit a copy of an existing published story in that dev channel, then authenticate `POST /admin/channel-feedback/preview/:storyId/:messageId` to bind the exact dev message. The endpoint only records that binding; it cannot send messages or accept arbitrary channels, drafts, or pages. The canonical published story must already exist. Callback processing checks the configured channel, published story, and exact message, so adding buttons to an unbound preview is insufficient. Dev clicks use the canonical article and the clicking user's normal feedback state; they do not change the canonical publication's message ID or page. Avoid using a test click on an account whose feedback you intend to leave untouched.

`GET /admin/reading` uses the existing admin authorization and exposes polling freshness, binding status, indexed/readable/parsed/failed counts, per-topic coverage, truncated-source count, deep-page count, and access capability/counts without user IDs. `POST /admin/reading/send` sends one preview batch to the configured owner only.

One poller fetches updates. Normal reading operations run FIFO; channel feedback is handled without waiting for a model request, including callbacks arriving in a later poll. At most 25 normal updates are pending; saturation pauses fetching until a slot opens. The persisted offset is claimed before side effects to avoid automatically repeating an ambiguous send after a crash. A crash before a callback transaction commits can leave the click unapplied and unanswered; this is not an exactly-once guarantee. After commit, duplicate callback receipts replay the original notification. Raw user messages and credentials are not logged.

The deterministic interaction tests cover identity rejection, five-item lists, selection versus preference changes, repeated bookmarking, revision-aware undo, old-batch replies, saved-list pagination, database reopening, title-only exclusion, polling replay protection, and text-grounded historical import. Public-path tests separately reject model/source/deep-page calls, exercise owned recommendation callbacks, and verify that revoking AI access preserves public reading. Limiter tests cover burst and refill behavior, notice suppression, restart persistence, recommendation cooldown, and the UTC+8 daily reset.

This document describes the implementation contract. Local tests do not establish that public recommendations or their limits are deployed. Live acceptance must separately confirm the deployed commit, public Telegram recommendations and feedback, denied AI access for an ungranted reader, archive counts, and Railway health. No deployment or live acceptance is claimed by this document.
